Section 80C Deductions: Maximize Your ₹1.5 Lakh Tax Break

By DJ Associates  ·  April 2025  ·  5 min read

Section 80C of the Income Tax Act is the most widely used tax-saving provision in India — yet most taxpayers do not fully utilize it. If you are under the old tax regime, you can claim up to ₹1.5 lakh in deductions. Here is how to use every rupee wisely.

What Qualifies Under Section 80C?

The following investments and expenses are all eligible for the ₹1.5 lakh deduction limit:

  • Employee Provident Fund (EPF) — your contribution, not employer's
  • Public Provident Fund (PPF) — 15-year lock-in, fully tax-free returns
  • Equity Linked Savings Scheme (ELSS) mutual funds — shortest lock-in of just 3 years
  • Life Insurance Premiums — for self, spouse, and children
  • National Savings Certificate (NSC) — 5-year government-backed scheme
  • 5-Year Tax Saver Fixed Deposits — available at all major banks
  • Sukanya Samriddhi Account — for girl children below age 10
  • Principal repayment on home loan
  • Children's tuition fees — for up to two children, school/college fees only
  • Senior Citizens Savings Scheme (SCSS) — for those above 60

ELSS vs PPF: Which is Better?

ELSS funds offer the shortest lock-in period (3 years) and historically the highest returns among 80C options — but they carry market risk and returns are not guaranteed. PPF is risk-free with sovereign backing and fully tax-free maturity, but locks your money for 15 years with limited premature withdrawal options.

For most working professionals under 45, a combination of both makes sense: ELSS for growth and PPF for stability. For conservative investors or those nearing retirement, PPF and NSC are more suitable.

Beyond 80C: Additional Deductions to Stack

Many taxpayers stop at 80C, but there is significantly more available under the old regime:

  • Section 80D — Health insurance premium: up to ₹25,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizens)
  • Section 80E — Interest on education loan: entire interest amount, no ceiling, for 8 years
  • Section 80TTA — Savings account interest exemption: up to ₹10,000
  • Section 24(b) — Home loan interest: up to ₹2 lakh on self-occupied property
  • Section 80CCD(1B) — NPS contribution: additional ₹50,000 over and above the 80C limit

Old Regime vs New Regime: Which Should You Choose?

These deductions are available only under the old tax regime. If your total eligible deductions — including 80C, 80D, home loan interest, and HRA — exceed ₹3 to 3.5 lakh, the old regime is likely more beneficial. If your deductions are lower, the new regime's reduced slab rates may save you more tax.

The best approach is to calculate your tax liability under both regimes before deciding. A small difference in deductions can sometimes swing the decision either way.

💡 Planning tip: The deadline to make 80C investments for FY 2024–25 was March 31, 2025. But start planning now for FY 2025–26 — spreading investments throughout the year avoids the year-end rush and lets your ELSS SIPs compound longer.
