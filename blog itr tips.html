ITR Filing for Salaried Individuals: 7 Tips to Get It Right This Season

By DJ Associates  ·  April 2025  ·  5 min read

Every year, thousands of salaried employees either miss deductions they are entitled to or make errors that invite scrutiny from the Income Tax Department. With the ITR filing season upon us, here are seven practical tips to file accurately — and make the most of your return.

1. Choose the Right ITR Form

Most salaried individuals with income only from salary and one house property should file ITR-1 (Sahaj). If you have capital gains from mutual funds or stocks, you will need ITR-2. Filing the wrong form is a common mistake that delays processing and can result in a defective return notice.

2. Cross-Check Your Form 26AS and AIS

Before you begin, download your Form 26AS and Annual Information Statement (AIS) from the Income Tax portal. Every TDS deduction by your employer, bank interest, or any other payer should match what is in these documents. Discrepancies must be resolved before filing — mismatches trigger automated notices.

3. Don't Miss House Rent Allowance (HRA)

If you pay rent but forgot to submit rent receipts to your employer, you can still claim HRA exemption while filing your ITR directly. Keep your rent receipts and the landlord's PAN handy if annual rent paid exceeds ₹1 lakh — your employer will need the PAN for TDS, and so will your ITR.

4. Declare All Bank Accounts

The ITR form requires you to list all bank accounts held during the financial year — not just the one where your salary is credited. Omitting accounts is a red flag during processing and can delay your refund.

5. Report Interest Income

Savings account interest, fixed deposit interest, and even recurring deposit interest are taxable. Many salaried filers omit these, which creates mismatches with AIS data. Include all interest income under "Income from Other Sources" and claim the ₹10,000 deduction under Section 80TTA for savings account interest.

6. E-Verify Within 30 Days

Filing your ITR is only half the job. You must e-verify it within 30 days via Aadhaar OTP, net banking, or other approved methods. An unverified return is treated as invalid — as if you never filed at all.

7. File Before July 31 to Carry Forward Losses

If you have capital losses from stocks or mutual funds that you want to carry forward to offset future gains, you must file on or before the due date. Filing late forfeits this benefit entirely, which can cost you significant tax savings in coming years.

💡 Pro tip: Even if your employer has deducted TDS correctly, a CA review of your ITR can often uncover additional deductions — especially under Sections 80D, 80E, and 24(b) — that many salaried employees overlook year after year.
